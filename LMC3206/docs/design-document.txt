BETWEEN TWO LANGUAGES — GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT
LMC 3206 / Fall 2026 / Working draft, November 13, 2026

CONCEPT
A short browser visual novel about An, a student who speaks Vietnamese at
home and English at school. A group project brings An's family and friends
into contact. The player chooses how to explain an ordinary question, ask
for time to find a word, and talk about belonging. The central idea is that
communication includes relationships, intentions, and audience expectations
as well as the words themselves.

AUDIENCE AND EXPERIENCE
Designed for desktop browsers, with a responsive mobile layout. English
translations are available for every Vietnamese dialogue line. The intended
audience includes monolingual readers curious about bilingual experiences.
The target is 10–15 minutes, pending timed human playtests. There are no timers,
language quizzes, correctness scores, or required Vietnamese knowledge.

CORE LOOP
Read a short conversation; optionally consult a translation; choose one of
two or three ways to respond; read a distinct reaction; optionally inspect a
context note; carry a reflection into the journal. Players can go back to
compare the consequences of another choice. Save/resume supports pauses.

EPISODE 1 — HOME: A SEAT AT THE TABLE
Dinner with Mom and Jules. Decisions concern a meal question, a preferred
form of address, and declining a second helping. Objective: distinguish a
close rendering of words from the interpersonal work those words do. The
host explicitly states her preferred address and respects the guest's no.

EPISODE 2 — SCHOOL: LOOKING FOR A WORD
A project meeting with Jules and Minh. Decisions concern expressing ngại,
including a friend after switching languages, and finishing a thought.
Objective: explore expression and collaboration without treating a pause
or a language switch as proof of deficient fluency.

EPISODE 3 — BETWEEN WORLDS: IN MY OWN WORDS
An shares the project with family and friends. Decisions concern framing,
personal boundaries, and the last line of an audio project. Objective:
explore audience and identity while allowing privacy and different ways
of being understood. Connection, agency, and listening are equally valid
closing emphases. The audio project is fictional; the game plays no audio.

STRUCTURE AND PRESENTATION
Three episodes, nine decisions, three options per decision, 27 authored
reaction sequences. Branches rejoin at the next scene. The final decision
changes the closing passage, and the journal preserves the full chosen
route. Warm paper, muted green, rust, and original SVG environments create
a quiet reading space. HTML, CSS, and JavaScript use the repository's Vite
build. All dialogue is stored in editable JSON. No backend is required.

EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION
These scenes are fiction, not collected testimony. Research is secondary
and currently includes public source summaries and publication records.
Read and document at least three sources through Georgia Tech Library;
review all six full texts before finalizing annotations. Review Vietnamese
phrasing with an appropriate reader, recruit two playtesters, and record
observations rather than claiming unmeasured learning gains. Confirm the
instructor's required writing equivalence. See the other documents here.
