[1] Grosjean, François. 1989. “Neurolinguists, Beware! The Bilingual Is Not
    Two Monolinguals in One Person.” Brain and Language 36(1): 3–15.
    https://doi.org/10.1016/0093-934X(89)90048-5
    Publisher abstract:
    https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0093934X89900485

The abstract contrasts a view of bilinguals as two separate monolinguals
with a holistic account of bilingual speakers. This provides a useful
challenge to a game built around perfect equivalence between languages.
Design connection: school.word and school.pause let An search, rephrase,
or collaborate without a fluency penalty. Limitation: the paper's
neurolinguistic focus does not establish the cause of this fictional
character's hesitation or a general rule about Vietnamese-English speech.
Read the article before extending this argument in the report.

[2] Gumperz, John J. 1982. Discourse Strategies. Cambridge University Press.
    https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511611834
    Publisher chapter summaries:
    https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/discourse-strategies/46D4D801BCC1FF7E6E31B2A19E45E92B/listing

The available summaries discuss conversational inference and switching
between linguistic systems within an exchange. Their emphasis on how
participants interpret speech provides a starting point for treating
switching as social action. Design connection: school.switch makes a
Vietnamese joke meaningful between An and Minh, then asks how Jules can
join the moment. The published examples described in the summary concern
other language pairs. The scene is a design application, not a reproduction
of Vietnamese data from this book. Prioritize chapters 4 and 6 for reading.

[4] Bucholtz, Mary, and Kira Hall. 2005. “Identity and Interaction: A
    Sociocultural Linguistic Approach.” Discourse Studies 7(4–5): 585–614.
    https://doi.org/10.1177/1461445605054407
    Publisher abstract:
    https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1461445605054407

The abstract presents identity as produced through linguistic interaction,
including positions and relationships that emerge locally. This helps frame
identity as something more situated than a fixed pair of language-specific
selves. Design connection: identity.friends lets An describe differences,
question a friend's assumption, or decline to explain. None is ranked as
more authentic. The article supplies a theoretical framework, not evidence
that these particular options represent all bilingual students. Reading
the full examples will help refine the final report's use of the framework.
